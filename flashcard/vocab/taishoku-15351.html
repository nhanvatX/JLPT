Learn JLPT N2 Vocabulary: 退職 (taishoku)

November 8, 2017   Learn Japanese N2 Vocabulary

[Ảnh: Learn JLPT N1 Vocabulary: 退職 (taishoku)]

Kana: たいしょく
Kanji: 退職
Romaji: taishoku

Type: Noun, するverb
Meaning: retirement, resignation
JLPT level: N1

Example sentences:

皆退職したか、別の職に変わっていた。
minna taishoku shita ka, betsu no shoku ni kawatte ita.
They had all been pensioned, or had passed on to some other employment.

まあ、中佐くらいまでなら昇進できるだろうし、２０年も従軍したら半額の恩給を受けて退職するのが普通だと言われてもいた。
maa, chuusa kurai made nara shoushin dekiru darou shi, nijuunen mo juugun shitara hangaku no onkyuu o ukete taishoku suru no ga futsuu da to iwarete mo ita.
Count on maybe becoming a lieutenant colonel and retiring with twenty years of service at half pay, I was told.

[Ảnh: 退職 (taishoku)]

ユウスケの父親は、退職した事務員です。
Yusuke’s father is a retired clerk.
Yūsuke no chichioya wa, taishoku shita jimu-indesu.

いまでは、退職した友人が周囲に大勢いる。
I have many friends in retirement now.
Imade wa, taishoku shita yūjin ga shūi ni taisei iru.

祖父は退職する年齢をとうに過ぎていった。
My grandfather was way past retirement age.
Sofu wa taishoku suru nenrei o tōni sugite itta.

自営業者は個人退職金口座に入金する義務はなく、また臨時被雇用者も年金を払っていない。
The self-employed were not obliged to contribute to Personal Retirement Accounts, and the casually employed do not contribute either.
Jieigyō-sha wa kojin taishokukin kōza ni nyūkin suru gimu wa naku, mata rinji hi koyō-sha mo nenkin o haratte inai.

ベビーブーム世代が退職年齢に達するにつれて、毎年、学区は経験豊かな教員を失っており、次の１０年には増大する入学者数に対応するだけで２００万人の教員を補充する必要がある。
bebiibuumu sedai ga taishoku nenrei ni tassuru ni tsurete, maitoshi, gakku wa keiken yutaka na kyouin o ushinatte ori, tsugi no juunen ni wa zoudai suru nyuugakushasuu ni taiou suru dake de ni hyaku man nin no kyouin o hojuu suru hitsuyou ga aru.
Each year, school districts are hemorrhaging experienced teachers as the Baby Boomers reach retirement, and two million teachers must be recruited in the next decade just to meet the needs of rising enrollment.

高齢者が路上で餓死してかまわないというなら話は別だが、なんらかの形で彼らの退職金をまかなわなくてはなるまい。
koureisha ga rojou de gashi shite kamawanai to iu nara hanashi wa betsu da ga, nanraka no katachi de karera no taishokukin o makanawanakute wa naru mai.
Unless we’re willing to see seniors starve on the street, we’re going to have to cover their retirement expenses one way or another.
