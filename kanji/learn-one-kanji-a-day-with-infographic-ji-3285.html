Learn one Kanji a day with infographic: 字 (ji)

September 10, 2016   Infographic, Kanji and Vocabulary

[Ảnh: Learn one Kanji a day with infographic: 字 (ji)]

MEANING:

character, letter, word, section of village

HOW TO READ:

On: ji
Kun: aza, azana, -na

HOW TO REMEMBER:

A child (子) born to a Japanese house (宀) is usually given Kanji characters for his/her name.

COMPOUNDS:

漢字 (kanji): Chinese characters
数字 (suuji): numeral, digit
文字 (moji): letter (of alphabet)
略字 (ryakuji): abbreviation
名字 (myouji): surname, family name
一字 (ichiji): letter, character
字幕 (jimaku): subtitles, captioning
欠字 (ketsuji): omitted word
十字 (juuji): cross, cruciform
字体 (jitai): type, font
誤字 (goji): misspelling, typo
字引 (jibiki): dictionary
字句 (jiku): words and phrases
活字 (katsuji): printing type
字数 (jisuu): number of characters
植字 (shokuji): typesetting
字書 (jisho): (character) dictionary
字義 (jigi): the meaning of a word
字下げ (jisage): indentation
頭文字 (kashiramoji): initials
一文字 (ichimonji): straight line
生字引 (ikijibiki): walking dictionary
小文字 (komoji): lower case letters
活字体 (katsujitai): printed form
女文字 (onnamoji): woman’s handwriting
文字通り (mojidoori): literally

EXAMPLE SENTENCES:

Cは何の略字だろう。
shii wa nan no ryakuji darou.
What does C stand for?

彼女は自分の頭文字を壁に書いた。
kanojo wa jibun no kashiramoji o kabe ni kaita.
She wrote her own initial on the wall.

文字がゆっくりと現われてきた。
moji ga yukkuri to arawarete kita.
Words formed themselves slowly.

これはきっと字引を見て書いたのだと思う。
kore wa kitto jibiki o mite kaita no da to omou.
I’m sure the word was taken out of a dictionary.

この紙は字を書いてから、ここのところが破りとってある。
kono kami wa ji o kaite kara, koko no tokoro ga yaburitotte aru.
The paper is torn away at the side here after the writing was done.

印刷された文字がはっきり浮き出てきた。
insatsu sareta moji ga hakkiri ukidete kita.
The printed words stood out clearly.

山田が大きな紙に数字を書きこんでいる。
yamada ga ookina kami ni suuji o kakikonde iru.
Yamada is entering up figures on large sheets of paper.

あなたはその土地の名を息子さんの名字になすったのですね。
anata wa sono tochi non na o musukosan no myouji ni nasutta no desu ne.
So you took the name of the county as a surname for your son.
