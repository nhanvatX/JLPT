Learn one Kanji a day with infographic: 裂 (retsu)

June 2, 2018   Infographic, Kanji and Vocabulary

[Ảnh: Learn one Kanji a day with infographic: 裂 (retsu)]

MEANING:

split, rend, tear

HOW TO READ:

On: retsu
Kun: sa.ku, sa.keru, -gi.re

JLPT LEVEL: N1

COMPOUNDS:

裂く (saku): to tear, to rip up
破裂 (haretsu): explosion, rupture
裂地 (kireji): cloth, fabric
爆裂 (bakuretsu): exploding
分裂 (bunretsu): split, division, break up
決裂 (ketsuretsu): breakdown, rupture
滅裂 (metsuretsu): in chaos, incoherent
亀裂 (kiretsu): crack, crevice
引裂く (hikisaku): to tear up, to tear off
裂ける (sakeru): to split, to tear, to burst
裂け目 (sakeme): tear, rip, rent
核分裂 (kakubunretsu): nuclear fission

EXAMPLE SENTENCES:

考えようとすると、頭に血がのぼって破裂しそうになる。
kangaeyou to suru to, atama ni chi ga nobotte haretsu shisou ni naru.
When I try to think, I feel as though my head were flooding with blood.

ＮＳＣは、リビアへの介入の是非をめぐって分裂していた。
enuesushii wa, ribia e no kainyuu no zehi o megutte bunretsu shite ita.
The National Security Council remained divided on the wisdom of intervening in Libya.

次の日、交渉は決裂した。一般地方自治体労働者組合がストライキに加わった。
tsugi no hi, koushou wa ketsuretsu shita. ippan chihou jichitai roudousha kumiai ga sutoraiki ni kuwawatta.
The next day negotiations collapsed. The General and Municipal Workers’ Union joined the strike.

大地に囲まれた巨大な裂け目から、地中の奥底に向かって巨大な水のカーテンのような滝がなだれ落ちている。
daichi ni kakomareta kyodai na sakeme kara, chichuu no okusoko ni mukatte kyodai na mizu no kaaten no you na taki ga nadare ochite iru.
From a gigantic rift in the land, an enormous curtain of water plunges into a deep chasm in the ground.
これを引き裂いたら、羽根が四方八方に飛んでいくでしょう。二度と枕のなかには戻せないのよ。
kore o hikisaitara, hane ga shihou happou ni tonde iku deshou. nido to makura no naka ni wa modosenai no yo.
If I tear this open, the feathers will fly to the four winds, and I could never get them back in the pillow.

これぞアメリカの精神で最上のものではないかと思った。逆向きの証拠はいろいろあっても、争いで分裂した国に一体感を回復することは可能と信じる大胆さ。
kore zo amerika no seishin de saijou no mono dewa nai ka to omotta. gyakumuki no shouko wa iroiro atte mo, arasoi de bunretsu shita kuni ni ittaikan o kaifuku suru koto wa kanou to shinjiru daitansa.
That was the best of the American spirit, I thought—having the audacity to believe despite all the evidence to the contrary that we could restore a sense of community to a nation torn by conflict.
